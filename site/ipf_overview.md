# Open Therapeutics #002 — Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis

## Summary of the open evidence record

This is a research record, not medical advice. It grades published evidence on one scale and
names what is missing. Nothing here establishes that a treatment is effective, safe or
appropriate for any patient. It has not been peer reviewed. Treatment decisions belong to a
qualified clinician with the full patient context.

Scope is idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis only. Evidence from other fibrotic lung diseases appears
only where it is labelled indirect evidence.

### 1. Where treatment stands

IPF is a progressive fibrosing interstitial pneumonia of unknown cause, defined by a usual
interstitial pneumonia pattern. Pooled global incidence is 5.8 per 100,000 (95% CI 4.8–6.8) and
prevalence 17.7 per 100,000 (95% CI 14.0–21.5). Registry cumulative mortality is 5%, 24%, 37% and
44% at years one to four, and the most advanced GAP stage carries a hazard ratio of 4.64 against
the least advanced. The frequently quoted median survival of about four years comes from 1998
data, predates antifibrotic therapy, and is not current prognosis.

Two antifibrotic drugs have been standard care for a decade, and two more agents have since
reported positive phase 3 lung-function results. What all of them do is slow the rate at which
forced vital capacity declines. The largest 52-week between-group difference in FVC decline
documented in any phase 3 IPF trial is 130.1 mL. Every active arm of every positive trial still
declined; the least-declining active arm reported anywhere in the record still lost 21.5 mL over
26 weeks. No trial has documented recovery of lost lung function, and no human trial endpoint in
this record measures reversal of established fibrosis.

No drug has randomised evidence of prolonging survival in IPF. The only survival evidence in the
record is observational, for lung transplantation, where a randomised comparison is not feasible.
Whether a treatment-induced change in the rate of FVC decline translates into longer survival is
unresolved — patient-level decline predicts death, but that is a prognostic association, not
evidence that changing the slope changes the outcome. This one unanswered question sits underneath
every positive verdict below.

Acute exacerbation occurs in 18.6% of patients over three years and carries about 29.5%
in-hospital mortality. No intervention in this record prevents or treats it with adequately
powered randomised evidence.

### 2. What the landscape looks like when everything is graded on one scale

279 interventions were graded on a single scale, from approved drugs to abandoned programmes,
supportive care, procedures and traditional formulas. The distribution is the central finding of
this record:

| verdict | n | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Works | 7 | evidence of benefit on one named outcome dimension |
| Uncertain | 51 | one direction indicated, evidence cannot carry the claim |
| Failed | 17 | adequately tested and negative on the endpoint tested |
| Harm outweighs benefit | 9 | harm demonstrated or benefit–risk judged negative |
| Poorly studied | 195 | the evidence needed to judge it does not exist |

**Only 7 of 279 interventions have evidence of benefit, and each is graded on one dimension
only.** Four are drugs graded on lung function: nintedanib (adjusted annual FVC change −114.7 mL
versus −239.9 mL with placebo), pirfenidone (47.9% relative reduction in the proportion of patients
declining by ≥10 percentage points of predicted FVC or dying at 52 weeks, positive in two of three
phase 3 trials), nerandomilast (−114.7 mL, 95% CI −141.8 to −87.5, at 52 weeks; its mortality and
exacerbation endpoints were not significant, and its approval is a label across progressive
pulmonary fibrosis rather than IPF-specific) and inhaled treprostinil (median 52-week FVC change
−49.9 mL, 95% CI −79.2 to −19.5). One is a symptom drug: nalbuphine ER reduced objective cough
frequency in two randomised trials of six weeks or less. Two are non-pharmacological: pulmonary
rehabilitation and exercise training improve exercise capacity and quality of life in the short
term, with durability beyond about six months unestablished; and lung transplantation, the only
intervention here with evidence of extended survival, from registry data.

**195 are poorly studied, which is not the same as ineffective.** These are interventions with no
IPF-specific controlled human result to grade — abandoned programmes, single-arm or non-clinical
studies, supportive measures generalised from wider interstitial lung disease, and traditional
preparations whose trials are not in the databases this record could reach. The record says what
is absent; it does not convert absence into either failure or promise.

**Traditional and historical interventions were included and graded on the same scale as
everything else.** 14 rows in the graded landscape are traditional or historical interventions.
None reaches a benefit verdict, and the reason is not that the evidence was weighed and found
wanting — for most of them the evidence could not be reached at all (see section 5).

### 3. The therapeutic ceiling: what the best available evidence does and does not reach

Grading twelve therapeutic dimensions against the best documented result in each makes the shape
of the field explicit. Slowing decline is well documented. Everything downstream of it is not.

| dimension | best documented result | what remains unachieved |
|---|---|---|
| Rate of FVC decline | 130.1 mL 52-week between-group difference | every active arm still declines |
| Preventing progression | 47.9% relative reduction in the proportion progressing | progression has never been prevented outright |
| Recovery of lost lung function | least-declining arm −21.5 mL over 26 weeks | no arm has reported sustained positive FVC change |
| Reversal of established fibrosis | none; no endpoint measures it | everything, including a validated measure |
| Acute exacerbation | pooled RR 0.63 (95% CI 0.53–0.76) with antifibrotics, predominantly observational | an adequately powered randomised demonstration |
| Cough | 60.2% highest documented placebo-adjusted reduction in objective cough frequency | durability beyond six weeks, independent replication |
| Dyspnoea and quality of life | no intervention exceeds the relevant minimal important difference | any demonstrated improvement in how patients feel |
| Functional capacity | no positive pivotal result from a disease-directed drug | a demonstrated exercise-capacity benefit from a drug |
| Survival | no randomised evidence for any drug | a randomised mortality demonstration |
| Durability | randomised comparison stops at about 52 weeks | any randomised evidence beyond one year |
| Definitive treatment | transplantation, 5-year survival 55.2% | candidacy, organ supply and age limit it |
| Cure or durable remission | none reported in any retrieved human evidence | everything |

Twelve unmet needs were registered from this ceiling. Every one of them is recorded as unaddressed
or open. The two that no intervention in any tradition currently targets are recovery of lost lung
function and reversal of established fibrosis — and for those two, no trial endpoint exists that
would measure success even if a candidate appeared. Five candidate endpoints for those dimensions
were specified during this record; none is validated, and the smallest validation step for each is
a re-analysis of imaging or trial data that already exists, not a new trial.

### 4. The negative results

77 interventions are in the negative-results register, kept because a failed programme is part of
what is known. They do not fail in the same way, and the record separates the reasons:

- **45 stopped before a result was established** — recruitment, funding, sponsor decision,
  operational or pandemic disruption. These are not evidence of no effect.
- **10 where harm exceeded benefit**, including the three-drug regimen of prednisone,
  azathioprine and N-acetylcysteine, stopped for increased death and hospitalisation. That
  randomised harm finding applies to the combination; it does not transfer to corticosteroid
  monotherapy, whose harm magnitude in IPF remains confounded by indication in every observational
  study that has examined it.
- **22 were null or worse than their comparator on the endpoint they were designed to test.**
  Three of those have no posted between-arm analysis at all.

Two interventions are conditionally recommended against for treating IPF in the current guideline:
antacid medication and antireflux surgery.

The record also distinguishes eight agents whose nulls came from trials too small or too short for
the null to be decisive, from those adequately tested and negative. Whether the former represent
absence of effect or absence of power is unresolved, and is recorded as such rather than as
failure.

### 5. Evidence that exists but cannot be graded, and evidence that cannot be found

This is the section with no counterpart in the first record in this series, and it is the largest
single limitation on everything above.

**Results that exist but cannot be graded.** A sweep of every IPF trial with posted results on
ClinicalTrials.gov — 116 trials — found postings that contain outcome tables but no between-arm
analysis, and postings with no located publication. 23 graded rows now carry an explicit
evidence-access state marking them as registry-only or as a posted result that cannot be graded.
The sweep produced eleven corrections to the record (C24–C34), including interventions regraded as
Failed on the strength of their own posted results, and duplicate rows merged. Registry-only
evidence stays identifiable in the published data; it is not silently mixed with published
evidence.

**Results that cannot be found.** Of 239 completed interventional IPF studies in the registry,
90 have posted results and 149 do not. For 110 of those 149, no result of any kind could be
retrieved, and 97 are past the twelve-month reporting window; the remaining 39 have a likely
publication whose primary outcome could not be verified. Searching by registry identifier located
publications for only 7 of the 110, and none of those publications reported the trial's missing
primary clinical outcome. The missing results are missing, not
mis-indexed. Retrieving them is the largest single addition available to this record, and it
carries no implication about what they would show.

**Evidence behind a language and access barrier.** Six English-indexed syntheses of
Chinese-language randomised trials were located, together summarising dozens of trials — one
pooling 63 randomised trials, others 20, 23 and 11, and an overview of reviews covering 15,550
participants. Almost none of the underlying interventions appear in the graded landscape, because
CNKI, Wanfang, VIP and SinoMed are subscription-gated and the Chinese trial registry refuses
automated access. This is a measured coverage gap, not a verdict: it means a substantial body of
randomised evidence has not been examined, and it credits that evidence with nothing. Closing it
requires a person with database access, and the first step is risk-of-bias assessment of full
texts, before any question of efficacy is entertained.

### 6. The conflicts left unresolved

12 conflicts were registered; 3 were resolved by supersession or by retrieving a missed source,
and 9 remain open. They are published unresolved rather than reconciled into a single conclusion.
The consequential ones:

- **Whether FVC is a valid surrogate for mortality** — the endpoint-level uncertainty that
  propagates to every positive verdict in the record.
- **N-acetylcysteine** — an overall null in an adequately sized randomised arm against a post hoc
  genotype-defined subgroup signal.
- **Corticosteroid monotherapy** — randomised harm evidence for a combination regimen against
  observational data in which pooled mortality risk (RR 1.03, 95% CI 0.85–1.25) is compatible with
  no effect, all of it at very low certainty and none of it free of confounding by indication.
- **Autoantibody reduction in acute exacerbation** — an unblinded open-label positive against a
  blinded null in a different setting.
- **Two positive results held at verification** — a registry posting with an internal
  implausibility, and a posted result that cannot be analysed as posted.

### 7. What remains open

**This record promoted no research lead.** 41 candidate signals were screened against six
prespecified screens; none met the promotion bar. That is the actual outcome, not an omission, and
Phase 5 — deep investigation of surviving leads — was recorded as skipped because there was
nothing to investigate.

The candidates are published in full as investigated candidates, each with its final screening
status and the reason it did not pass. 37 were rejected, and for 6 of those the record retained a
retrieval, analysis or measurement action the candidate had prompted even though it was rejected
as a therapeutic lead;
**4 are held at verification** — their key result could not be checked against primary full text,
so they are neither promoted nor rejected. A held candidate is a verification state, not a
positive finding. The four are: N-acetylcysteine in a genotype- or telomere-defined subgroup, a
traditional-medicine granule with one small randomised abstract, autoantibody reduction for acute
exacerbations, and a single-site nasal-spray cough result.

The most common reason a candidate failed was the absence of any controlled human signal to test —
not an unfavourable result. Missing, unretrieved or inaccessible evidence never functioned as a
signal in either direction.

Twelve actions carry forward, and eight remain open. None of them is a proposal for a new clinical
trial. They are: obtaining the results of a completed genotype-stratified trial that is unreported
and would resolve the record's best-documented subgroup question; obtaining primary full texts for
the held findings; individual-participant re-analysis of existing trial data for the surrogacy and
adherence questions; examination of corticosteroid harm in existing registry cohorts; and access
to the Chinese-language literature. The record's own judgement is that the smallest useful next
step is retrieval and re-analysis of evidence that already exists, not new trials.

### 8. How to check this record

Everything above is derived from the published registers, and every statement is meant to be
checkable against them:

- **89 graded statements** in the treatment map, each with its section, outcome dimension,
  evidence tier, caveat and sources.
- **283 intervention rows** (279 distinct, 4 flagged duplicates) with verdict, tier, the verbatim
  key result, the reason for the verdict and the evidence-access state.
- **77 negative results**, **12 conflicts**, **41 screened candidates** with the six screen
  definitions, **12 unmet needs**, **10 open questions**, **7 named limitations**.
- **38 corrections** in the corrections log, each naming what was claimed, what replaced it, the
  basis and the effect. Later corrections supersede earlier statements throughout; where a
  published row was corrected, the correction id travels with the row.
- The registers of what could not be reached: the retrievability register (149 trials), the
  registry-results sweep (116 trials), the located-publications register, and the Chinese-language
  coverage register.
- A machine-readable payload, `ipf_record.json`, built from those registers by a script that is
  published with it, plus a QA report of the automated checks run on both.

Two publication-stage normalisations were needed and are disclosed rather than applied silently:
two intervention rows carried a verdict label in their evidence-tier column, and six internal
treatment-map identifiers were each used by two rows. Both are recorded in the payload's
`meta.normalisations`, and no research file was rewritten to accommodate the published view.

What would change this record: the release of the unreported genotype-stratified trial, primary
full texts for the four held findings, and an examination of the Chinese-language randomised
literature by someone with database access. Short of those, the picture stays as it is — a disease
where decline can be slowed, nothing yet restores what has been lost, and most of what has been
tried was never tested well enough to know.
